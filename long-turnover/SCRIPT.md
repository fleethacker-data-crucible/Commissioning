# THE LONG TURNOVER: proposal film, script v1

Source: `comms/proposals/long-turnover/` (concept `long-turnover.html`, pilot `long-turnover-pilot.html`). Every number and claim below comes from those two documents. Nothing is added that they do not say.

**Format:** fully produced, fictional dramatization wrapped around a real proposal. About 4:10. Cinematic, not a screen demo. 1080p master; the first pass is a 480p animatic (see Production plan).
**Tone:** quiet, human, a little salty. A pitch that makes a funder feel the problem before they hear the ask.
**Look:** night-watch palette. Deep navy, sodium amber, brass gold, one cold blue for "the agent". Slow lens, long shadows, real faces. Motion graphics are drawn like ship's instruments, not corporate slides. Same navy and gold as the Fleet demos, so it belongs to the series.
**Honesty cards (on screen, required):** an opening card "A fictional dramatization. People and events are invented." and, on the chart, "Illustrative model, not measured data." The ask and the budget use the proposal's exact figures and its "confirm every price with a vendor quote" caveat.
**Captions:** toggleable soft track plus `.srt`, never burned in.

## Voice lines (exact, for the voice tool)

HARLAN (narration, 20 lines above condensed to these takes):
1. "A watch turnover takes fifteen minutes. A career takes fifteen years."
2. "Most of what makes a senior person valuable never gets written down. Who to call. Which alarm always lies. Why the workaround from 2019 is still there. And when they rotate, retire or transfer, it walks out the gate with them."
3. "What if the job could be handed over the whole way, with her consent, from her first week to her last? The Long Turnover is a way to capture it. With her consent. On her machine. Anonymized, then distilled by generative models. And counted only once she confirms it. Not a surveillance product. An agent she helps build, and signs."
4. "It has a service record. It shadows. It co-pilots. It sits sea trials against her own past cases. It relieves her, and it advises, but a human decides. Then it goes emeritus, with the date of its knowledge on every answer. And one day it is decommissioned, on purpose. Opt-in. Revocable. Never used to rate or rank her. Anonymized before any model sees it. Every claim traced to its source. A named human decides. And her name stays on what she taught it."
5. "Passive capture is good at what people do. It is poor at why. The why lives in the hard calls, and she does not know which ones mattered. Neither does the system. So we ask a different question. Can wearable sensors tell us when a hard decision is being made? Not what she is thinking. When. The sensor only picks the moment. The answer is hers. The sensors never read thoughts, never judge skill, and the raw signals are deleted after each session."
6. "Phase one is small on purpose. Ninety days. Four developer volunteers. Two veteran-and-successor pairs, in an isolated lab. Nothing identifiable leaves the building. Setup. Calibration. Real work, with questions timed by sensors against questions on a schedule. And a successor test: can the next person finish the veteran's task using only what was captured? Five questions, each with a measure."
7. "The request is equipment only: about seven and a half to eight thousand dollars. Labor is the team's own time. It ends in one of three ways. Go: field pilot. Pivot: keep the headsets, drop the extra sensors. Stop: keep the weekly questions, no sensors. Each outcome is useful."
8. "If it works, the road runs on: a field pilot, a software relief agent, and, years away, a robot that carries the expert's judgment as well as their motions. Always with a named person accountable. This part is not part of this request."
9. "We are asking for four decisions. Fund the equipment. Approve the quote request. Approve a ninety-day study with volunteers. And name the people who will hold us to our own guardrails."
10. "A watch turnover takes fifteen minutes. A career takes fifteen years. Hand it over the whole way. Let's start now."

CALLOWAY (in-picture, sparse): "Ask me." (on the label, read aloud once at 0:15 as a recorded voicemail greeting; optional). Optional line at 2:22: "That one, yes. Here is why."
TOMAS: "Ask you." (0:15)
RELIEF: "Loop 4. In winter this alarm is a known false trigger. Confirmed by Chief Sullivan, eleven months ago. Please verify before you ignore it."

Check on accuracy: nothing above says the sensors read minds, judge skill or rate anyone; the chart is labeled illustrative; the robot phase is labeled not requested and not funded; equipment is the proposal's option B and its stated range; the eye-tracker quote and Neon glasses are not in that cap.
